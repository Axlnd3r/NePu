## Claim

The hypothesis: over the long run, fee revenue from NEAR Intents grows until buybacks and gas burn cover new NEAR issuance. The evidence for and against it follows. The reading today:

**Not yet.** In the 30 days to 2026-10-31, buybacks and gas burn covered 12 cents per dollar of new NEAR issued (CR_float 0.120 in USD, 0.120 in NEAR units). At today's take rate and issuance daily Intents volume would have to reach $989.9M, 7.6x its $130.4M 30-day average, before capture covers issuance.

That multiple does not depend on how Intents volume is counted. The revenue that reaches the buyback wallet is measured on chain; counted at half or twice DefiLlama's volume, break-even becomes $494.9M or $2.0B a day and the multiple stays 7.6x. Read it as the growth in buyback revenue that break-even needs; the dollar volume is only as good as the volume definition.

It does depend on the NEAR price. The 7.6x values issuance at the latest price ($4.05 on 2026-10-30); the 12 cents use each day's price (mean $4.92 over the window), and on that basis the gap is 9.2x. Issuance is fixed in NEAR while fee revenue is earned in dollars, so a higher NEAR price raises the bar.

Buybacks are not burns: the NEAR bought back is held in a wallet, so only gas burn lowers total supply (CR_supply 0.013 on 2026-11-01). The thesis is falsifiable: the numeric conditions that would change this reading are listed below.

## What it means for a holder

- Net new supply: gross issuance ran at 2.5% of total supply a year over the 30 days to 2026-10-30; after buybacks and gas burn, 2.2% was left as new float. Bought-back NEAR is held, not burnt, so total supply itself grows by gross issuance less burn.
- A yardstick, not a price view: at the latest price total supply is valued at 308x the yearly revenue reaching the buyback wallet. Coverage reaches one when that multiple is 40.6x; the ratio of the two is the 7.6x above. The multiple moves with revenue, the issuance rate and the price; none of them is forecast here.

## Supporting evidence

Evidence that capture can catch up with issuance:

- The measurement holds: on-chain revenue agrees with DefiLlama's in 10 of 10 complete months, the worst gap being 11.0% against a tolerance of 15%.
- The buyback wallet does receive revenue: the highest 30-day coverage measured so far is 0.166, on 2026-10-01.
- The buyback take rate has risen: its monthly value ran from 0.0150% to 0.0362% of volume across the complete months observed.
- Issuance policy can help: the HSP-029 proposal (issuance down to 1.6% a year) would cut the required volume by a factor of 1.58x, and sending all protocol revenue to buybacks by a further 1.51x, leaving 3.18x to come from volume growth.
- The House of Stake vote on HSP-029, read on chain from `vote.dao` at block 223,734,663, has closed with status `Timelock`: 99.97% of the veNEAR cast went to one option (turnout 62.3%), and its timelock ends 2026-11-16T16:48:08Z. Validators still have to adopt the ramp, so it is a scenario here, not a measurement.
- Required growth is finite: reaching break-even in 3 years at 2.5% issuance needs the 30-day volume to grow 97% a year.

## Contrary evidence

Evidence that it will not, or not soon:

- Only 65.5% of the protocol revenue that reaches NEAR wallets reaches the buyback wallet; the rest sits in collection wallets that have not paid out.
- Per 10,000 dollars of Intents volume, fees were 16.01 dollars, protocol revenue 5.48 and the buyback wallet received 3.62, while new NEAR worth 33.91 was issued.
- What can be seen of Intents volume is shrinking: the Explorer (1Click only) covered 48.7% of DefiLlama's volume in 2026-07 and 27.4% in 2026-10.
- The buyback wallet's own activity (NearBlocks, 2026-04-27 to 2026-10-31): it received 1,626,879 NEAR from `intents.near`, 510,068 NEAR moved in from the other revenue wallets and 8,227 NEAR from 10 other accounts, and sent 12,000 NEAR out. The net daily change behind CR counts the NEAR from other accounts as buybacks (0.5% of what it counts), and cannot see an outflow on a day with a larger inflow. The activity nets to 2,133,175 NEAR against 2,133,521 NEAR in the balance series CR uses.
- DefiLlama's Intents volume looks counted on both sides of a swap. From 2026-10-24 to 2026-10-30 the stablecoin legs of swaps settled on `intents.near` (token_diff events, BigQuery), counted once, came to 24.0% of DefiLlama's volume, while stablecoins were 53.3% of 1Click volume in 2026-10: about what counting both legs would give. The dollar break-even above may be about half as large; the multiple does not change.
- Volume is concentrated: in 2026-10 one channel carried 40.1% of 1Click volume (channel HHI 0.214), and declared app fees to NEAR wallets explained 21.9% of on-chain revenue, down from 68.8% in 2026-07.
- Intents revenue is not network usage: from 2026-04-26 to 2026-10-30, calls to `intents.near` were 4.8% of all function calls and its daily signers 0.45% of daily active accounts (distinct transaction signers).
- Network usage did not follow Intents: comparing the two halves of the 26 complete weeks, daily active accounts fell 33% and transactions fell 6%, while Intents volume rose 72% and the NEAR price rose 62%. No cause is attributed.
- The track's example hypothesis, that Intents volume leads network usage better than the token price, is not supported out of sample: Daily active accounts: Intents volume, lag of 3 weeks, r=0.21 in training and r=-0.11 on 8 test weeks (p=0.79); NEAR price, lag of 3 weeks, r=0.52 in training and r=-0.35 on 8 test weeks (p=0.34). Transactions: Intents volume, lag of 3 weeks, r=-0.50 in training and r=0.15 on 8 test weeks (p=0.71); NEAR price, lag of 1 week, r=0.38 in training and r=0.42 on 10 test weeks (p=0.22). Volume does not lead usage out of sample more reliably than price; with this few weeks that is a null result, not proof of absence.
- The growth that makes capture look inevitable comes from the launch phase: mean monthly log growth was 0.191 over 16 months but 0.006 after the launch. Extrapolated for 60 months it implies a median daily volume of $13.8T, which is outside anything observed; in the flat scenario, which does not use it, 28% of 10,000 simulated paths have coverage of at least one in month 60.
- Developer activity does not lead revenue (a null result): Lag 0 had the largest correlation in training (r=-0.72). In the test months it is r=0.90 on 5 pairs (permutation p=0.19). That cannot be told apart from chance with this few points; this is a null result.
- The NEAR price in USD is held constant at its latest value in every run. This isolates volume, take rate and inflation; it is not a forecast and no price level is predicted. The 0.5x and 2x runs are sensitivities to that assumption.

## Why public estimates differ

Published figures for how much of NEAR's issuance is covered range widely. Most of the spread is definition, not data. The same days, on this project's data:

- Buyback wallet + gas burn, 30 days (the headline's basis): 12.1% (2026-10-01 to 2026-10-30).
- Buyback wallet + gas burn, 90 days: 13.2% (2026-08-02 to 2026-10-30).
- All three revenue wallets + gas burn, 30 days: 17.8% (2026-10-01 to 2026-10-30).
- DefiLlama holders revenue (as NEAR) + gas burn, 30 days: 17.6% (2026-10-01 to 2026-10-30).
- All three revenue wallets, no burn, since 1 January, in NEAR: 13.7% (2026-01-01 to 2026-10-30).
- Gas burn only (total-supply view), 30 days, in NEAR: 1.4% (2026-10-01 to 2026-10-30).
- revenue.near.org: revenue / emissions since 1 January, as published: 15.9% (to 2026-10-30). On the same days its emissions agree with our gross issuance (26,853,326 against 26,888,612 NEAR) but its revenue is 4,275,735 NEAR against the 3,697,105 NEAR the three revenue wallets received. The page books revenue in monthly steps, does not document its method and calls its figures illustrative, so the gap is reported here, not explained.

The headline uses the strictest buyback basis on purpose: only the buyback wallet holds NEAR bought back; the other revenue wallets have not paid out.

## Limitations

- The proof: the SHA-256 of the snapshot manifest is committed to NEAR testnet at block 276,017,917. It shows that the hash existed no later than that block; it does not show that the data is correct. Testnet carries no economic security and keeps no promise to retain its history.
- Scope of the channel data: 1Click transactions only.
- Results are conditional on the stated assumptions, not forecasts; no price is predicted.
- The growth sample is short and dominated by the launch phase: see growth.n_months, growth.mean_all and growth.mean_after_launch.
- The historical and decay scenarios inherit a mean growth set by the launch phase; volume_p50_usd_per_day shows the volume each run implies. A probability close to 1 there rests on that volume, not on observed behaviour after the launch.
- The tau range is the lowest to highest of a few complete months and the latest month is its maximum, so the drawn tau is below the current one on average and the flat scenario's median CR starts below the measured CR.
- The buyback take rate has few complete months and was rising; its range is crude.
- Real supply grows with issuance, which lowers CR slightly: the runs are a bit optimistic.
- Buybacks are not burns (CLAUDE.md rule 6): CR is the float view, buyback plus gas burn.
- Month 0 at the latest price differs from the measured CR_float because the buyback is USD converted at that price; at the mean price of the same 30 days the model reproduces it (start.cr_model_month0_at_window_price). CR in every run is therefore price-dependent.
- Correlation is not causation; lead/lag results are reported out of sample, including null results.
- DefiLlama is a third-party source and its last days are not final.
